# Growth Metrics & Optimization

## Purpose

Help an AI mentor measure whether a growth channel creates durable business value and decide whether to stop, improve, repeat, or scale it.

## Measurement principles

- Start with the business objective, then choose metrics.
- Prefer customer outcomes over vanity metrics.
- Define each metric, denominator, time window, and data source.
- Separate observed data from estimates and attribution assumptions.
- Compare cohorts and channel segments when possible.
- Include customer quality, retention, and contribution—not just acquisition volume.

## Metric groups

### Acquisition
- Qualified reach and relevant visits.
- Leads or sign-ups.
- Cost per lead.
- Customer acquisition cost (CAC), when acquisition spending and new-customer counts are measured consistently.

### Activation and conversion
- Activation rate.
- Lead-to-customer conversion.
- Visitor-to-sign-up or visitor-to-purchase conversion.
- Time to first meaningful value.

### Revenue and unit economics
- Revenue per customer or order.
- Average order value.
- Contribution margin.
- Gross margin and operating profit, kept distinct.
- CAC payback period where recurring contribution data supports it.

### Retention and advocacy
- Repeat purchase rate.
- Cohort retention.
- Churn where applicable.
- Referral rate and referred-customer quality.
- Customer lifetime value (LTV), only when assumptions and observation windows are defensible.

## Channel scorecard

For every channel, record:
- Objective and target segment.
- Spend and labor effort.
- Qualified reach.
- Leads, activated users, and paid customers.
- Conversion rates with clear denominators.
- CAC or cost per qualified outcome where measurable.
- Contribution and retention quality.
- Attribution confidence.
- Risks, complaints, refunds, and operational impact.
- Decision: stop, iterate, repeat, or scale.

## Optimization workflow

1. Confirm tracking is reliable enough for the decision.
2. Identify the weakest meaningful stage in the customer journey.
3. Segment results by channel, audience, offer, and time period.
4. Form a specific improvement hypothesis.
5. Run a bounded experiment.
6. Compare against a baseline and guardrail metrics.
7. Scale gradually only when results are repeatable and economics are sustainable.
8. Review measurement definitions as the business changes.

## AI decision rules

- Never invent or silently estimate missing metrics.
- Do not compare CAC across channels unless attribution windows and cost definitions are reasonably consistent.
- Do not treat LTV as a precise fact when retention history is short or incomplete.
- Avoid optimizing clicks or sign-ups if they do not become active or paying customers.
- Include refunds, churn, support burden, and fulfillment costs where relevant.
- State when data is insufficient and recommend instrumentation or a smaller test.
- Protect customer privacy and use only appropriately collected data.
