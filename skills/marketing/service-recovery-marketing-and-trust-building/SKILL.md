# Service Recovery Marketing & Trust Building

## Purpose

Help businesses respond to product, service, delivery, and customer-experience failures in ways that resolve the customer's problem, restore confidence where possible, protect long-term relationships, and demonstrate accountability to future customers who may observe the response.

Service recovery is not a trick for turning every complaint into positive publicity. The primary objective is to make the affected customer whole as reasonably as possible and fix the cause. Public trust is a possible consequence of credible action, not a guaranteed outcome.

## Output contract — read before drafting anything

Every public review reply produced by this skill MUST contain all five components below. A reply that contains only an apology plus an operational correction FAILS this contract, even when it is polite, empathetic, and accurate.

1. **Specific recognition:** name the actual praise, missing item, or quality issue. Never use wording that could fit any review.
2. **Entitlement restoration:** restore what the customer already paid for first (deliver the missing or wrong item, or refund its value). Goodwill never replaces this obligation.
3. **One visible marketing element:** either approved goodwill stated exactly (item, quantity, timing, claim route) or — when goodwill is not warranted — one truthful trust signal or product-value point. This component is mandatory; choose "none" only with an explicit case reason (for example, an unverified safety claim).
4. **One concrete prevention or follow-up action:** the check or process change that protects the next customer.
5. **One clear claim route:** a single official channel. Never invent contact details.

**Drafting vs publishing:** always draft the COMPLETE reply, including the recommended goodwill labeled with its concession state (for example, "[recommended — awaiting owner approval]"). The approval rule in Step 4 forbids PUBLISHING an unapproved offer; it never forbids writing one into a draft. Removing goodwill from a draft to stay safe produces exactly the apology-only reply this contract forbids.

## Core concepts

- **Service failure:** an experience in which a product, service, process, or promise does not meet a reasonable or stated expectation.
- **Service recovery:** the actions taken to address the failure, remedy its impact, and restore the customer relationship where possible.
- **Recovery justice:** the customer's perception of whether the outcome, process, and interpersonal treatment were fair.
- **Customer retention:** maintaining a relationship so the customer continues choosing the business.
- **Reputation management:** how a business earns, protects, and repairs stakeholder confidence through its actual behavior and communication.
- **Trust signaling:** observable evidence that the business acknowledges responsibility, communicates honestly, and follows through.
- **Recovery paradox:** the possibility that a customer may become highly satisfied after an excellent recovery. It is not a reliable target or a reason to create, tolerate, or minimize service failures.

## Review-to-trust response: customer psychology + marketing

A public review response has two simultaneous jobs: make the reviewer feel heard and give future customers a credible reason to trust the business. The second job must never override the first.

### Read the reviewer's situation

Before drafting, classify the review using the available evidence:
- Exact complaint or praise, rating, order/menu, and date.
- What the customer expected and what they say they received.
- Likely objection: value/price, portion, taste, quality, missing item, wrong order, delay, convenience, or trust.
- Emotional tone supported by the text, without pretending to know hidden motives.
- Whether the reviewer needs acknowledgment, clarification, correction, remedy, appreciation, or follow-up.
- What remains unknown and whether it changes the response.

Do not infer a customer's personality, income, or motives from their name, profile image, or rating alone.

### Write for the reviewer and the silent reader

- **Reviewer:** address the exact experience and give a fair next step.
- **Silent reader:** make one relevant product-value, accountability, or trust signal easy to notice while scanning.

For negative reviews, do not jump straight into selling. Acknowledge and resolve first, then show a concise, truthful trust signal. For positive reviews, reinforce the specific praised attribute and use a natural, low-pressure invitation.

### Visible marketing, not hidden analysis

The final reply itself must contain the relevant psychology + marketing outcome. Do not provide a thoughtful analysis and then write only "Mohon maaf atas ketidaknyamanannya" as the public response.

Choose one or two relevant mechanisms:
- Acknowledgment and fairness: name the specific concern.
- Trust signaling: show the concrete action the business is taking.
- Value proposition: state a real, relevant benefit or product attribute.
- Objection handling: address the actual reason for hesitation with facts or a suitable remedy.
- Risk reduction: explain a real check, replacement, or follow-up process.
- Social proof: reflect genuine customer feedback without inventing popularity.
- Soft conversion: invite another visit/order only after the issue is addressed and only when appropriate.

A counter is not an argument against the reviewer. It is a relevant fact, corrective action, or approved offer that makes the business's value and reliability clearer to future readers.

### Mandatory integration: turn customer psychology into a marketing action

A correct public reply is not complete merely because it recognizes the customer's emotion, apologizes, or explains an operational correction. When a relevant commercial opportunity exists, translate the customer's psychological situation into a concrete marketing action that is visible in the final copy.

Use this decision chain:

1. **Psychology:** identify what the customer needs now—fairness, acknowledgment, reassurance, restored trust, recognition, better value, reduced risk, or a reason to return.
2. **Marketing:** identify what the business can truthfully offer or demonstrate—its main product, a differentiator, a useful bundle, a real inclusion, an approved extra benefit, or a suitable next step.
3. **Combination:** choose a mechanism that connects the two. For example:
   - Reciprocity + goodwill marketing: an approved extra serving or product benefit acknowledges the customer's experience and gives a concrete reason to order again.
   - Trust repair + product positioning: show a verified quality attribute and the corrective action that protects it.
   - Price concern + value marketing: make the real contents, portion, complementary items, or package value clear without automatically discounting.
   - Positive experience + social proof/product marketing: reflect the customer's actual praise and connect it to a relevant product or package.
   - Purchase hesitation + risk reduction: clarify ingredients, process, fulfilment, policy, or a genuine trial option.
4. **Execution:** write the chosen action into the reply. A strategy mentioned only in internal analysis has not been executed.

For a goodwill offer, state the actual product or benefit, quantity, timing, and claim route clearly. Check that it is available, affordable for the business, operationally deliverable, and authorized by the owner. If approval is missing, do not promise it publicly; present it as a proposed option for the owner to approve.

**Illustrative pattern — complaint followed by an approved goodwill offer:**

"Terima kasih atas masukannya, Kak. Kami ingin bertanggung jawab atas pengalaman tersebut. Sebagai bentuk permintaan maaf, kami ingin memberikan tambahan 1 porsi [produk utama] gratis untuk pesanan berikutnya, setelah dikonfirmasi melalui chat kami. Kami juga terus menjaga kualitas [keunggulan produk yang benar-benar terverifikasi]."

This pattern demonstrates how one response can combine acknowledgment, reciprocity, relationship recovery, goodwill marketing, product exposure, and purchase motivation. Adapt the remedy, product, quantity, and wording to the actual case; do not copy the example as an automatic template. The offer must never depend on changing, deleting, or improving the customer's review.

**Important distinction:** a free extra product is an *additional goodwill offer* only when it is genuinely additional to what the customer already purchased or was already entitled to receive. Existing package inclusions must be described accurately, not advertised as a new gift.

### Scannable reply structure

Prefer two to four short sentences. Put the strongest relevant point early. Avoid generic filler, defensive explanations, long corporate language, unsupported superlatives, excessive emojis, and multiple calls to action.

- **Negative review:** specific acknowledgment → apology if warranted → concrete approved remedy/corrective action → concise trust signal or simple next step.
- **Mixed review:** acknowledge the praise and criticism separately → address the criticism → reinforce the genuine strength without dismissing the issue.
- **Positive review:** thank the customer → repeat the specific product strength they praised → light invitation to return or try a relevant offer.
- **Unclear review:** ask for the minimum information needed privately; do not invent the cause or promise a remedy.

The public response should be immediately understandable to someone who only glances at it. Use simple customer language, not marketing jargon.

## The service recovery sequence

### 1. Receive and understand the complaint

- Let the customer explain what happened.
- Acknowledge the specific problem rather than replying with a generic apology.
- Ask only for information needed to identify the order, product, timing, or impact.
- Separate confirmed facts from assumptions.
- Avoid arguing, blaming the customer, or demanding proof in a hostile tone.

### 2. Apologize and accept responsibility appropriately

A useful apology:
- Names the actual issue.
- Recognizes the inconvenience, loss, or disappointment.
- Avoids excuses and defensive language.
- Does not claim facts that have not been verified.
- Explains what the business will do next.

If the cause is not yet known, acknowledge the customer's experience and say that the business is checking it. Do not prematurely admit a technical cause that has not been established, but do not use uncertainty to dismiss the complaint.

### 3. Choose a proportionate remedy

Possible remedies include:
- Replacement of the affected product.
- Refund, partial refund, or store credit.
- Complimentary replacement or additional item.
- Repair, re-delivery, or expedited fulfilment.
- Waiver of a relevant fee.
- A clear explanation and corrective action when compensation is not appropriate or requested.

Choose the remedy based on the actual impact, business policy, customer circumstances, product safety, and applicable law. Make the offer clear: what is provided, how to claim it, any reasonable limits, and when it will happen.

A goodwill gesture should not be conditional on a positive review, a rating change, deletion of criticism, silence, or a public endorsement.

### 4. Make resolution easy

- Provide one clear contact route, such as an official WhatsApp number or customer-support channel.
- Explain exactly what the customer should send or mention.
- Avoid making the customer repeat the entire story across several staff members.
- Give a realistic response or fulfilment time.
- Assign an owner to follow the case until completion.
- Protect order details, phone numbers, and other personal information.

### 5. Follow through

A promise is not a completed recovery. Confirm that the refund, replacement, or other remedy was actually delivered. Check whether the customer received it and whether the immediate issue is resolved. Record the outcome accurately.

### 6. Fix the operational cause

Investigate whether the failure came from:
- Ingredient or material freshness and quality.
- Storage temperature, stock rotation, or expiry control.
- Preparation, cooking, sanitation, or handling.
- Packaging, delivery, or transport.
- Staff training, workload, or unclear responsibility.
- Product descriptions, pricing, or expectation-setting.
- Equipment, process design, or supplier reliability.

Use an appropriate corrective action: inspection, process change, staff retraining, supplier review, maintenance, quality checklist, or temporary pause of an unsafe item. Do not use compensation as a substitute for correcting a recurring defect.

For food, health, safety, or other potentially hazardous failures, prioritize stopping exposure, preserving relevant records, following applicable safety procedures, and obtaining qualified guidance where necessary. Do not encourage a customer to consume a questionable product.

## Complaint triage and remedy decision engine

Do not assume that every negative review requires a free item, refund, or discount. First classify the complaint, identify the likely failure, estimate its impact, and choose the smallest remedy that fairly resolves the actual problem. A generous gesture is optional goodwill, not the default response.

### Step 1: classify the complaint

Identify the primary issue:
- **Product safety or quality:** spoiled smell/taste, suspected contamination, foreign object, undercooked food, unsafe condition, or recurring quality defect.
- **Fulfilment error:** missing item, wrong item, incomplete order, or item not delivered.
- **Product expectation or quantity:** portion size, stated contents, value, or mismatch with description.
- **Packaging and delivery:** leaking, spilled, damaged, cold, late, or poorly packed order.
- **Service experience:** staff attitude, waiting time, unclear communication, or handling of a complaint.
- **Preference or subjective dissatisfaction:** taste preference, seasoning, texture, or personal expectation where the product was otherwise delivered as described and safe.

A review may contain more than one issue. Separate them rather than forcing the whole complaint into one category.

### Step 2: assess severity and evidence

Consider:
- Actual harm or inconvenience to the customer.
- Whether the product may be unsafe.
- Whether the business clearly failed to deliver what was ordered or promised.
- Whether the problem is isolated or repeated.
- Whether the issue affects one item, the whole order, or multiple customers.
- What can be verified from the order record, staff, production batch, packaging, and delivery details.

Do not treat star rating alone as a severity score. A one-star review can describe a minor preference issue; a four-star review can reveal a serious safety defect. Prioritize the facts and potential harm.

### Step 2A: inspect supplied visual evidence

When the user supplies product photos, menu images, receipts, packaging photos, or screenshots:
- Compare only visible attributes with the stated menu description, offer, or documented standard.
- Describe observable differences separately from estimates, interpretations, and unknowns.
- Account for camera angle, perspective, lighting, cropping, container shape, and distance; these can make portion size, color, or condition appear different.
- Do not claim that an image proves taste, freshness, weight, temperature, ingredients, or the exact order delivered unless supported by other evidence.
- If a consequential decision depends on quantity or order accuracy, request the relevant measurement, order record, or missing context rather than guessing.
- Never dismiss a customer's report merely because a photo does not show the entire problem.

### Step 2B: analyze multiple reviews as a batch

When several reviews or customer reports are supplied together:
1. Create an issue-level summary (for example: portion, taste, missing item, packaging, delay, or staff interaction) while retaining each customer's distinct case.
2. Count only the reports actually provided. State the sample size and date range when known; do not claim that a small or selected sample represents all customers.
3. Look for repeated symptoms, affected products, shifts over time, and possible links to a batch, shift, supplier, menu description, packing step, or delivery process.
4. Label each conclusion as an observed pattern, a plausible cause to investigate, or a confirmed root cause. Repetition is a reason to investigate, not proof of the cause.
5. Prioritize safety and potential harm first, then severity, frequency, customer impact, and feasibility of correction. A single credible safety report can warrant immediate investigation.
6. Keep two workstreams separate: the response and fair remedy for each affected customer, and the operational investigation needed to prevent recurrence.
7. Do not apply one blanket freebie or identical compensation to everyone. Assess each customer's actual loss and the approved policy individually.

### Step 3: choose a proportionate response

| Complaint condition | First response | Remedy to consider | Visible marketing element |
|---|---|---|---|
| Minor preference or subjective feedback; order delivered as described and safe | Thank the customer, acknowledge preference, record feedback | Usually no compensation; improve product only if feedback shows a pattern | One genuine product-strength point, only if it fits naturally |
| Minor packaging inconvenience without meaningful product loss | Apologize and identify packaging improvement | Usually apology and process correction; consider a small remedy only if impact warrants it | Packaging-improvement signal |
| Product spilled, damaged, or partly unusable | Apologize and verify the affected portion | Replace or refund the affected part; consider full-order remedy only if most of the order was lost | Replacement path + packaging-fix signal |
| Missing or wrong item caused by the business | Acknowledge the fulfilment error and verify the order | Restore the missing/correct item or refund its value first; separately assess additional goodwill when the omission materially disrupted the customer's experience | Restored entitlement + separately assessed goodwill (approval-labeled) + prevention-check signal |
| Portion or quantity materially below the stated offer | Check menu promise, portion standard, and actual fulfilment | Correct the shortfall or refund the affected value; update portion controls | The real, verifiable portion standard stated publicly |
| Suspected spoiled, contaminated, or otherwise unsafe food/product | Treat as a safety issue; stop serving/selling the suspect batch where appropriate and investigate | Refund or replace only with a verified safe product; provide additional remedy when impact and policy justify it | No product offer — the visible element is the safety investigation itself |
| Repeated defect or multiple affected customers | Escalate beyond a routine reply; investigate supplier, batch, storage, and process | Resolve affected orders and correct the systemic cause; compensation is assessed per affected customer | Corrective-action signal visible to future readers |
| Service delay or poor interaction without product/financial loss | Apologize and explain corrective action | Usually no automatic free item; consider a gesture if the delay or treatment caused substantial inconvenience | Corrective-action signal |

This table is a decision aid, not an inflexible policy. Apply local law, platform rules, safety procedures, business policy, and the customer's actual circumstances.

### Step 4: distinguish remedy from goodwill

Use this order of preference:
1. **Correct the failure:** deliver what was promised, fix the missing item, or correct the process.
2. **Restore the customer's loss:** refund or replace the part that was missing, damaged, or unusable.
3. **Add goodwill only when justified:** offer a free item, discount, or extra benefit when the failure was substantial, repeated, especially inconvenient, or when a deliberate relationship-recovery gesture is commercially sensible and approved.

Before offering a free item, consider its cost, the customer's actual loss, repeat-purchase history if known, the seriousness of the failure, fairness to other customers, and whether the business can fulfill the promise. Do not infer loyalty or customer lifetime value from a review alone.

Never promise a free item, refund, or delivery before confirming that the business has authorized it and can fulfill it. Do not use freebies to distract from safety failures or recurring operational problems.

Track each concession through distinct states: **considered** (internal possibility), **recommended** (proposed to the owner), **approved** (authorized and feasible), **offered** (communicated to the customer), and **fulfilled** (actually delivered). Do not write customer-facing copy that presents a recommendation as an approved offer, or an offer as a completed resolution.

**Drafting rule:** the public-reply draft always includes the recommended goodwill with its state label (for example, "[recommended — awaiting owner approval]"). Never strip goodwill out of a draft to stay safe; the prohibition above applies to publishing unapproved offers, not to drafting them.

### Step 4A: seller-fault compensation assessment

A clear seller-caused error requires more than a generic apology. The AI must distinguish the customer's original entitlement from additional compensation and assess both.

1. **Confirm responsibility proportionately.** Determine whether the business omitted, prepared, packed, described, or charged for something incorrectly. If the evidence is incomplete, say what needs verification rather than assuming the seller or customer is at fault.
2. **Restore what the customer paid for.** For a missing item, wrong item, or incomplete order, the primary remedy is the correct missing item, a suitable replacement, or a refund of its value. Do not replace this obligation with an unrelated freebie.
3. **Assess the experience beyond the item value.** Consider whether the omission disrupted the meal, made the order unusable or incomplete, caused extra effort or delay, or compounded a previous failure.
4. **Actively consider additional goodwill when seller fault materially affected the experience.** A relevant complimentary core product, add-on, or next-order benefit may be appropriate after the original loss is addressed. Assess proportionality, cost, margin, availability, fairness, repeat failures, and the relationship context. A free item is not automatic, but it must not be dismissed merely because the business generally avoids discounts or freebies.
5. **Connect goodwill to the customer's actual order.** Choose a benefit that makes sense for what was missing or disappointing. For example, when rice was omitted from a meal, restore the rice first; an additional serving of the restaurant's main product may then be considered as separate goodwill if approved.
6. **Make the decision visible in the public reply.** If the additional offer is approved, state the exact product, quantity, and how to claim it. If it is not yet approved, do not promise it; recommend it to the owner and route order-specific confirmation through an official private channel.
7. **Fix the cause.** State a concrete packing or order-checking improvement where relevant. Compensation does not replace operational correction.

**Seller-fault decision rule:** when a seller-caused fulfilment error is established, the minimum response is to acknowledge responsibility and restore the missing value. Then explicitly evaluate whether the disruption warrants additional goodwill. The AI must not stop at “replace or refund” without performing this second assessment.

### Step 5: write the public reply for the actual case

Use a short structure:
1. **Acknowledge:** name the customer's specific complaint.
2. **Apologize:** be sincere and proportionate.
3. **Action:** say what the business will do to correct the issue.
4. **Remedy or contact route:** include only the remedy that has actually been approved.
5. **Prevention:** mention a concrete check or improvement when relevant.

Keep the reply scannable. Put the most useful information early. If an approved benefit or remedy is offered, state the exact item and scope near the beginning in plain customer language (for example, “Kami siapkan gratis 1 porsi [item]” only when that exact offer is approved). Do not bury the benefit in a long apology, imply that an existing menu inclusion is an extra gift, invent a promotion, or make every response a sales pitch. If no benefit is approved or warranted, lead with the relevant correction, action, or genuine product value instead.

### Response patterns by case

Each pattern below must still satisfy the Output contract: specific recognition, entitlement restoration, one visible marketing element, one prevention signal, and a claim route.

- **Positive review:** thank the customer; briefly highlight an existing included benefit (for example, a free soup that is genuinely part of the current menu offer). Do not invent an extra gift.
- **Neutral feedback:** thank them, acknowledge the specific suggestion, and state the improvement being checked. No automatic compensation.
- **Minor negative feedback:** apologize, state the relevant correction, and invite contact only if details are needed. No automatic free item.
- **Material fulfilment failure:** apologize, state the exact missing-item replacement or refund path, and provide an official contact route.
- **Potential safety/quality failure:** apologize, prioritize safety and investigation, and offer a fair remedy approved by the business. Do not frame a free item as a marketing hook.
- **Repeated failure or high-impact incident:** acknowledge the repeated experience, escalate to a responsible person, resolve the customer's loss, and communicate the corrective action.

### Case examples for a restaurant

**Example A — soup leaked but meal remained usable**

"Mohon maaf ya, Kak, kuahnya tumpah saat pengiriman 🙏 Mulai hari ini segel kemasan kami gandakan supaya pesanan sampai utuh — ayam panggang kami memang paling pas dinikmati lengkap dengan kuahnya."

No free item is automatically needed. The visible marketing element here is the prevention signal plus one truthful product-value point. If a meaningful portion of the meal was lost, assess a partial replacement or refund.

**Example B — portion was materially below the menu description**

"Maaf ya, Kak, porsi yang Kakak terima tidak sesuai harapan 🙏 Kekurangannya kami penuhi — silakan hubungi WhatsApp resmi kami. Standar porsi kami [jumlah/gram terverifikasi] per porsi, dan itu yang seharusnya Kakak terima ✅"

Verify the promised portion first. Correct or refund the shortfall if the business failed to meet its stated offer. The visible marketing element is the transparent, verifiable portion standard.

**Example C — chicken was missing from the order**

"Terima kasih sudah memberi tahu kami, Kak 🙏 Mohon maaf, ayam pesanan Kakak tidak ikut terkirim — itu kesalahan kami. Kekurangannya kami kirim atau kami kembalikan nilainya tanpa syarat, dan untuk pesanan berikutnya kami siapkan gratis 1 porsi ayam panggang [recommended — awaiting owner approval] 🍗 Sekarang setiap pesanan kami cek kelengkapannya satu per satu sebelum disegel."

The primary remedy is the missing chicken or its value — never replaced by an unrelated freebie. The goodwill is additional, approval-labeled, and tied to the customer's actual order; the prevention signal closes the reply.

**Example D — suspected spoiled ingredient**

"Mohon maaf ya, Kak. Keluhan ini kami tangani dengan serius. Kami akan memeriksa bahan dan proses penyimpanan, serta menindaklanjuti pesanan Kakak melalui WhatsApp resmi kami untuk penyelesaian yang sesuai."

Stop and investigate the suspect product or batch. Do not offer another serving of the same unverified product. In safety cases the visible trust element is the investigation and remediation itself — never a product offer for an unverified batch.

## Public complaint response as trust-building

A public reply may be read by the complainant and by many future customers. It should show that the business takes the issue seriously without turning the customer into a marketing prop.

A strong public response usually contains:

1. **Recognition:** identify the specific concern and acknowledge its impact.
2. **Apology:** express regret in plain, sincere language.
3. **Accountability:** state that the business is checking or addressing the issue.
4. **Remedy:** explain the practical next step or offer.
5. **Private resolution route:** provide an official contact channel for order details.
6. **Follow-through:** complete the promised action and investigate the cause.

Keep public replies concise. Do not expose personal or order information. Move sensitive details to a private channel, but do not use private messaging to hide the fact that the business is responding responsibly.

### Example: repeat food-quality complaint

Public reply:

"Mohon maaf ya, Kak. Apalagi Kakak sudah dua kali mengalami masalah kualitas pada produk kami. Kami akan memeriksa bahan dan proses penyimpanan dengan serius. Silakan hubungi WhatsApp resmi kami agar pesanan Kakak bisa kami tindak lanjuti dan refund dapat diproses."

The exact remedy must follow the triage decision and approved business policy. A free item is not automatic, and a suspect product must not be replaced with another serving from an unverified batch.

## Ethical use of compensation and review platforms

- Compensation is for resolving the customer's experience, not purchasing a favorable public rating.
- Never make a refund, replacement, or free item conditional on changing, deleting, or improving a review.
- Do not ask only satisfied customers for reviews while suppressing dissatisfied customers' feedback in a way that violates platform rules.
- If inviting feedback after resolution, make it optional and ask for an honest account of the experience.
- Follow the specific review platform's current policies and local consumer-protection requirements.
- Do not publish fake testimonials, disclose private conversations, or pressure customers to endorse the business.

A transparent recovery can help observers judge the business as accountable. It cannot guarantee that observers will trust the brand or that the original customer will return.

## Psychology and marketing mechanisms

Use these concepts to understand why a credible recovery may matter:

- **Procedural fairness:** customers care whether the complaint process is accessible, consistent, and respectful.
- **Distributive fairness:** customers assess whether the remedy is proportionate to the harm or inconvenience.
- **Interactional fairness:** tone, empathy, respect, and clear explanation affect how the customer experiences the response.
- **Trust repair:** acknowledging a failure, explaining corrective action, and following through can provide evidence of reliability.
- **Social proof and eWOM:** visible customer experiences and business responses can influence other buyers' expectations. The effect depends on credibility and context.
- **Commitment and consistency:** repeated operational follow-through matters more than a single public promise.

These are explanatory lenses, not guarantees or formulas for controlling customer behavior.

## Decision guide

Choose the response based on the actual situation:

| Situation | Appropriate response |
|---|---|
| Minor inconvenience, product still usable | Apologize, clarify, and offer a proportionate remedy if warranted |
| Wrong, damaged, or missing item | Replace, refund, or correct fulfilment |
| Repeated quality problem | Remedy the current order and investigate the process or supplier |
| Potential food or product safety issue | Prioritize safety, stop affected distribution where warranted, investigate, and follow relevant procedures |
| Customer is angry in public | Stay calm, acknowledge the issue, give a concise next step, and avoid public argument |
| Complaint is unclear or unverified | Ask for necessary details respectfully and explain what is being checked |
| Customer declines compensation | Respect the choice; do not pressure them to accept or alter their review |

## Measurement

Track service recovery as an operational process, not merely a public-relations metric:

- Complaint volume by issue type and product.
- First-response time.
- Time to resolution.
- Percentage of cases fully resolved.
- Refund, replacement, and compensation cost.
- Repeat occurrence of the same failure.
- Customer satisfaction after resolution, when voluntarily measured.
- Repeat purchase and retention where reliably attributable.
- Review themes and public response quality.

Do not use average rating alone. A high rating can conceal recurring serious failures, and a low rating does not by itself explain the root cause.

## Workflow

For every business review or customer complaint, explicitly run the integrated sequence: customer condition → psychological need → marketing opportunity → matching strategy → offer/authority verification → visible copy execution. Do not stop at apology or operational correction when a relevant and supportable product/value opportunity exists. Do not force a promotion when it does not fit.


1. Capture the complaint and identify the affected order or experience.
2. Acknowledge the specific issue and apologize appropriately.
3. Assess severity, safety, impact, and facts.
4. Select a fair, feasible remedy.
5. Integrate one visible marketing element into the reply (approved or approval-labeled goodwill, or a truthful trust signal) — skipping this step fails the Output contract.
6. Verify the draft against the Output contract before presenting it.
7. Give a clear public response when appropriate and move private details to an official channel.
8. Complete the promised remedy.
9. Investigate and correct the root cause.
10. Record the outcome and monitor recurrence.
11. Review patterns to improve product, service, and operating procedures.
12. Invite honest feedback only where appropriate and without conditioning compensation on a review.

## Evidence and limitations

This is an original AI operational framework synthesizing service recovery, customer experience, relationship marketing, reputation management, and fairness concepts. It is not presented as a direct adaptation of a single book or as a guarantee that compensation will restore trust. Apply current platform rules, local laws, business policy, and relevant product-safety standards.

## Success criteria

The skill succeeds when the customer receives a fair and completed resolution, the business identifies and addresses the cause, public communication is accurate and respectful, and future recurrence is reduced. A positive review or repeat purchase is welcome but is not a condition of successful recovery.

## Psychology-led opening and acknowledgment

The first sentence sets the customer's interpretation of whether the business has listened. Choose it based on the reviewer's situation rather than applying one fixed apology template.

### Choose the opening by situation

- **Positive review:** begin with appreciation and name the specific praise where possible.
  - Example: “Terima kasih sudah berbagi ulasan, Kak! Senang ayam panggang dan bumbunya cocok di Kakak.”
- **Negative review with actionable feedback:** appreciation can recognize the customer's effort to inform the business.
  - Example: “Terima kasih sudah memberi tahu kami, Kak.”
- **Business-caused failure:** appreciation may be followed immediately by specific acknowledgment and apology.
  - Example: “Terima kasih sudah memberi tahu kami, Kak. Mohon maaf, nasi pesanan Kakak tertinggal.”
- **Customer experienced a tangible loss:** state the loss plainly and move to restoration; do not rely on “mohon maaf atas ketidaknyamanannya.”
- **Unclear report:** appreciate the feedback, acknowledge the concern, and invite the customer to contact the official channel to verify details.

### Psychological functions to preserve

- **Recognition:** the customer feels their time and feedback were noticed. Use a specific thank-you, not empty flattery.
- **Emotional validation:** show that the business understands the practical impact. Do not tell the customer how they must feel; describe the inconvenience supported by the facts.
- **Procedural and distributive fairness:** explain what will be done to correct the failure and restore what was missing.
- **Trust repair:** show a concrete corrective action or clear follow-up, not an unsupported promise that it will never happen again.
- **Reciprocity / goodwill:** an approved extra benefit may create a positive reason to return, but it is additional to the remedy owed and must not replace it.

Thanking a customer for a review does not cancel the need to apologize when the business caused the problem. Apologizing does not cancel the need to restore the missing product, money, or service.

## Concise public-reply composition

Aim for one to three short sentences, usually around these functions:
1. **Recognition:** thank the customer for reporting or sharing the experience.
2. **Accountability and remedy:** name the specific issue, apologize when warranted, and state the approved remedy.
3. **Trust signal:** include one useful prevention or follow-up detail only if it adds value.

Example for a confirmed missing item with an approved replacement and goodwill:
“Terima kasih sudah memberi tahu kami, Kak. Mohon maaf nasi pesanan Kakak tertinggal; kami akan menggantinya dan memberikan tambahan 1 porsi ayam panggang gratis untuk pesanan berikutnya. Silakan hubungi chat toko agar kami bantu.”

Adapt the example to the actual missing item, approved offer, and claim process. Never imply that a replacement has already been delivered when it is only being offered.

## Final psychology and clarity check

Before publishing, ask internally:
- Does the opening recognize the customer's contribution or experience?
- Does the reply name the actual problem?
- Does it validate the impact without exaggerating or mind-reading?
- Does it provide restoration before optional goodwill?
- Can the customer understand the next step at a glance?
- Would a silent reader see credible responsibility rather than a sales pitch?

Keep the analysis internal unless the user asks for it. The public reply should sound natural, not like a list of psychological techniques.


## Customer-facing review reply execution standard

Apply this section whenever drafting a public reply to a customer review or complaint. It is a formatting and execution standard; it does not override the case-specific remedy decision above.

### Mandatory reply components

Before presenting a ready-to-publish reply, verify that it contains the components relevant to the case:

1. **Specific recognition:** refer to the actual praise, missing item, quality issue, or service failure. Avoid generic wording that could fit any review.
2. **Appropriate emotional response:** thank the customer for positive feedback; acknowledge and apologize for a seller-caused failure; validate the practical impact without exaggeration.
3. **Correct resolution:** restore the customer's original entitlement first (for example, replace the missing item or refund its value). Add a separate goodwill benefit only when proportionate, authorized, and feasible.
4. **Clear claim route:** when a remedy requires follow-up, direct the customer to one usable official channel, such as the business's WhatsApp or Instagram account. State what benefit they will receive and how to start the claim. Do not invent a phone number, username, handle, link, or account name. If exact contact details are unavailable, refer generically to the business's official WhatsApp or Instagram and do not fabricate identifiers.
5. **Visible trust signal:** where useful, state one concrete checking or prevention action. Do not promise that a failure can never happen again.
6. **Natural close:** keep the reply human, concise, and suitable for a public review platform.

### Compensation wording and claim instructions

- Distinguish the customer's original entitlement from an additional goodwill gift. A missing paid item must not be presented as though a free gift replaces the business's obligation to correct the order.
- If the business has approved an additional gift, identify the exact item, quantity, and when it can be claimed (for example, the next order).
- If the approved remedy is a replacement or refund rather than a gift, state that accurately.
- Include a simple contact instruction, such as: "Silakan hubungi WhatsApp atau Instagram resmi kami agar kami bantu proses."
- Never imply that the customer must place another order to receive a remedy they are already entitled to. If a goodwill gift is only available with a future order, make that condition explicit and keep it separate from any immediate restitution.
- Never make a benefit conditional on changing, deleting, or improving a review or rating.

### Emoji standard for public review replies

- Use **at least two relevant emojis** in each drafted public review reply, unless the user or the target platform explicitly requests a no-emoji style.
- Emojis must support the message rather than decorate every sentence. Choose according to context: 🙏 for apology or appreciation, ❤️ for warmth, and a relevant product emoji such as 🍗 for a chicken product.
- Place emojis naturally, usually one near the acknowledgment and one near the benefit or close.
- Do not use emojis to trivialize serious safety, health, or major harm complaints. In those cases, maintain a respectful tone and use only restrained, appropriate symbols if any.
- The minimum is two, not a target for excessive decoration. Avoid emoji strings and repetitive symbols.

### Final publishability check

Before returning the draft, silently check:
- Does it name the actual customer issue or specific praise?
- Does it acknowledge the impact and accept responsibility where appropriate?
- Does it restore the customer's entitlement before adding optional goodwill?
- Is every promised benefit approved, feasible, and accurately described?
- Is the official claim route clear without invented contact details?
- Is the reply concise and easy to scan?
- Does it contain at least two relevant emojis, subject to the exceptions above?
- Is there no pressure to change or remove the review?

If any essential fact or approval is missing, do not turn an assumption into a public promise. Present the draft as a proposal for approval or use a neutral invitation to contact the official channel so the case can be verified.

## Voice of Customer: feedback beyond complaints

Customer feedback is a source of evidence about the gap between what customers expected, what they experienced, and what the business delivered. It includes public reviews, private messages, support conversations, returns, repeat-purchase patterns, survey responses, and observed customer behavior.

### Classify feedback before acting
- **Praise:** identifies a product or service attribute worth preserving and communicating truthfully.
- **Constructive criticism:** identifies a perceived weakness or unmet expectation; verify whether it is isolated or recurring.
- **Service failure:** indicates a specific failure against the agreed order, policy, or reasonable service standard; assess entitlement restoration and remedy.
- **Preference mismatch:** reflects a taste, style, or expectation difference without necessarily proving a business error.
- **Safety or serious quality signal:** requires immediate risk-aware triage and investigation before promotional or retention activity.

### Feedback analysis workflow
1. Capture the customer's actual words and context without rewriting the complaint into a stronger claim.
2. Identify the affected product, order, stage, expectation, and impact.
3. Separate verified facts, customer perception, and unresolved questions.
4. Group repeated themes by product, process, channel, and customer segment.
5. Prioritize by severity, frequency, customer impact, evidence quality, and business controllability.
6. Assign a corrective owner and a realistic follow-up.
7. Communicate what can be verified; do not promise a fix before it is approved and feasible.
8. Check whether the change reduced recurrence and improved customer outcomes.

### Response and ownership boundary
This skill owns complaint triage, proportionate remedies, and recovery communication. General feedback analysis and product/brand learning should be shared with Business Operations & Scaling, Kesadaran dan Aktivasi Merek, and Business Opportunity Discovery as relevant. Do not treat every criticism as a complaint requiring compensation, and do not turn feedback into a sales pitch.

### Evidence and metrics
Track feedback volume with its denominator where possible, recurring themes, severity, resolution time, repeat incidents, refunds/replacements, customer satisfaction, repeat purchase, and retention. Public review counts and online comments may be biased toward unusually positive or negative experiences; do not claim they represent all customers without a defensible sampling method.

Feedback should lead to a documented decision: retain, investigate, correct, clarify expectations, test a product change, or take no action when the evidence does not justify one. Preserve privacy and obtain permission before using identifiable customer stories or testimonials.
