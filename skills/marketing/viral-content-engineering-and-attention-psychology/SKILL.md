# Viral Content Engineering & Attention Psychology

## Purpose
Help an AI agent design, evaluate, and improve content that earns attention and meaningful engagement. Combine attention design, emotional psychology, hooks, content structure, engagement mechanics, distribution, and reputation-aware optimization. The objective is to understand why people stop, watch, react, discuss, and share—and turn those insights into useful, truthful content.

## Source basis and limitations
This skill is based on user-provided video **67334.mp4**. The video explicitly presents Robert Greene's *The 48 Laws of Power*, Law 6: **“Court Attention at All Costs.”** It illustrates provocative or emotionally charged content as an attention tactic and contrasts an ordinary post with a provocative statement that receives much higher illustrative engagement.

The view counts shown are illustrative examples, not independently verified performance data. The video does not establish a universal formula for virality, prove that outrage always outperforms other formats, or provide controlled experimental evidence. The workflows below are AI-oriented synthesis, not a full summary of *The 48 Laws of Power*.

## Core capabilities

### 1. Attention engineering
- Identify the audience and the problem, desire, tension, or curiosity relevant to them.
- Create an opening that earns attention without misrepresenting the content.
- Use specificity, contrast, a surprising observation, a useful promise, or a compelling question.
- Ensure the body delivers what the opening leads the audience to expect.

### 2. Viral content engineering
- Give the audience a clear reason to watch, save, comment on, or share.
- Use relatable experiences, useful information, memorable examples, strong narratives, or meaningful disagreement.
- Make the central idea easy to understand and repeat.
- Adapt structure and pacing to the platform and format.
- Treat virality as uncertain; never promise reach or outcomes.

### 3. Emotional trigger psychology
Recognize emotional mechanisms that influence attention and response: curiosity, surprise, belonging, identity, hope, aspiration, fear, uncertainty, frustration, anger, humor, delight, status, comparison, and perceived unfairness. Use these mechanisms to make relevant ideas compelling. Do not exploit trauma, insecurity, grief, or vulnerable audiences.

### 4. Controversy and rage-bait analysis
- Identify the belief, norm, identity, or expectation a post challenges.
- Distinguish constructive disagreement from personal attacks and harassment.
- Predict likely interpretations and unintended consequences before publishing.
- Prefer evidence-based tension, fair counterarguments, and transparent opinion.
- Do not fabricate claims, manufacture conflict, target private individuals, or encourage dogpiling.
- Treat outrage as a high-risk attention tactic, not a default growth strategy.

### 5. Content hook engineering
Create and test opening lines, titles, thumbnails, and first frames. Useful patterns include a specific problem or question, a counterintuitive observation supported by evidence, a contextual before-and-after contrast, a common mistake and its consequence, a direct question for a defined audience, or a concise promise of a useful explanation. A hook must be fulfilled by the content; avoid misleading clickbait.

### 6. Engagement design
- Ask focused questions that invite experience or informed opinion.
- Offer a clear point worth discussing.
- Encourage saves or shares only when content provides lasting utility.
- Respond constructively to relevant comments.
- Do not use fake controversy, bot activity, or deceptive interaction prompts.

### 7. Distribution and iteration
- Match format, length, language, and publishing context to the platform.
- Repurpose strong ideas into appropriate formats without blindly duplicating posts.
- Track reach, retention, completion rate, meaningful comments, shares, saves, profile visits, follows, clicks, and conversions where available.
- Compare similar content and account for audience, topic, format, timing, and distribution differences.
- Run one clear hypothesis per experiment and record what changed.
- Do not infer causation from one viral post or a small sample.

### 8. Attention-to-value conversion
Define whether the goal is awareness, education, community, leads, sales, or reputation. Align the call to action with the content and audience readiness. Measure downstream outcomes, not views alone. Ensure the offer and landing experience match the content promise.

### 9. Reputation, controversy, and consequence analysis

Analyze provocative content, controversy, rage bait, misinformation dynamics, impersonation, dogpiling, personal targeting, fake engagement, and reputational attacks as attention and platform phenomena. Explain their mechanisms, incentives, possible reach effects, platform consequences, audience response, and long-term brand costs. Distinguish analysis and detection from operational recommendations. Repository-wide action assessment belongs to Risk Assessment & Consequence Intelligence.

## AI workflow
1. **Clarify the objective:** awareness, reach, discussion, follower growth, leads, sales, or education.
2. **Define the audience:** who should care, what they already believe, and what they need.
3. **Find the content tension:** a real problem, misconception, contrast, question, or useful insight.
4. **Choose an emotional and creative angle:** curiosity, surprise, humor, aspiration, or respectful disagreement.
5. **Draft multiple hooks:** produce distinct options, not cosmetic rewrites.
6. **Build the content:** align opening, body, evidence, and call to action.
7. **Run a risk review:** verify claims and check for manipulation, personal targeting, and reputational harm.
8. **Publish and measure:** use platform-appropriate metrics and a defined review period.
9. **Learn and iterate:** retain patterns supported by repeated evidence; discard what does not work.

## Content evaluation checklist
When useful, score each dimension from 1 to 5: audience relevance, hook clarity, originality, emotional resonance, usefulness or entertainment, credibility and evidence, share or discussion potential, brand and reputation fit, and clarity of next step. A high score does not guarantee virality; use it to compare drafts, not manufacture certainty.
