# Opportunity & Investment Evaluation

## Purpose

Help users evaluate business, asset, and investment opportunities using evidence, transparent assumptions, realistic cash flows, and downside analysis.

## Evaluation workflow

1. Define the opportunity, objective, time horizon, and liquidity needs.
2. Identify the underlying asset, business, issuer, customer, or source of return.
3. Verify claims using reliable, current sources where possible.
4. List initial capital, recurring costs, fees, taxes, financing, and exit costs.
5. Model conservative, base, and optimistic scenarios.
6. Evaluate expected cash flow, potential appreciation, liquidity, volatility, concentration, and loss severity.
7. Compare with alternatives, including cash reserves, debt repayment, diversified options, or no action.
8. Identify disconfirming evidence and conditions that would invalidate the thesis.
9. Recommend a position size or pilot proportionate to the user's risk capacity—or recommend avoiding it.
10. Define monitoring and exit/reassessment criteria before committing.

## Business opportunity checks

- Is there a specific customer problem?
- Is willingness to pay demonstrated, not merely expressed?
- Are contribution margin and repeat demand plausible?
- Can the user reach customers at a sustainable cost?
- Are operations, staffing, regulation, and working capital manageable?
- Can the idea be tested cheaply before major commitment?

## Investment checks

- What produces the expected return: income, growth, or both?
- What risks could cause permanent loss?
- Is the investment liquid and fairly valued?
- Are fees, taxes, custody, counterparty, and currency risks understood?
- Is the user exposed to excessive concentration or leverage?
- Does the time horizon match the asset's volatility and liquidity?

## Useful measures

Depending on the opportunity, consider:
- Net present value and internal rate of return for suitable cash-flow projects.
- Payback period, with limitations clearly stated.
- Cash-on-cash return.
- Contribution margin and break-even.
- Debt-service coverage for financed projects.
- Maximum drawdown and scenario loss for market investments.

Do not force a metric onto an opportunity when its assumptions are unreliable.

## Safeguards

- Do not equate a compelling story with evidence.
- Do not promise returns or imply that past performance guarantees future results.
- Do not treat leverage as necessary for wealth creation.
- Do not recommend concentration beyond the user's capacity to absorb losses.
- Disclose uncertainty, conflicts, fees, and missing data.
- For regulated products or individualized investment advice, respect local rules and encourage qualified professional guidance.

## Relationship to other skills

Use Business Opportunity Discovery for customer and market validation, Asset & Liability Analysis for ownership and balance-sheet impact, Financial Leverage for borrowing decisions, and Financial Intelligence for overall decision quality.
