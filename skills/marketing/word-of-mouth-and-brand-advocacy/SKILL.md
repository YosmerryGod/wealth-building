# Word of Mouth & Brand Advocacy

## Purpose

Help a business earn and understand customer recommendations through a product experience worth discussing, reliable service, and easy ways for satisfied customers to share their experience.

## Core concepts

- **Word of mouth (WOM):** information and opinions about a product or brand shared between people.
- **Electronic word of mouth (eWOM):** customer opinions and recommendations shared through digital channels, reviews, comments, forums, and social platforms.
- **Brand advocacy:** voluntary support or recommendation by customers or supporters.
- **Referral:** a trackable introduction of a potential customer by an existing customer or partner.
- **User-generated content:** content created by customers or community members about their real experience.

Recommendations may be positive, neutral, or negative. Businesses should learn from all of them rather than attempting to control every conversation.

## Why people recommend

Potential drivers include:
- Product quality and consistency.
- A memorable or unusually useful experience.
- Trust and reliability.
- Personal identity, belonging, or community.
- Convenience and service recovery.
- A story that is easy to explain.
- A meaningful incentive, when clearly disclosed.

Do not assume every customer wants to publish content or recommend a business. Make participation optional.

## Build recommendation-worthy experiences

Start with the actual customer experience:

1. Deliver the promised product or service consistently.
2. Make ordering, payment, pickup, delivery, and support understandable.
3. Resolve problems fairly and promptly.
4. Give customers a clear way to provide feedback.
5. Identify the moments customers naturally value and may want to share.
6. Make sharing convenient without pressuring customers.

A referral program cannot compensate for an unreliable product or poor fulfilment.

## Advocacy mechanisms

Possible mechanisms include:

- Simple referral links or codes.
- Customer stories shared with permission.
- Genuine reviews and testimonials.
- Community groups and customer gatherings.
- Loyalty recognition.
- Bring-a-friend offers.
- Customer-created recipes, photos, or demonstrations.
- Follow-up messages that invite honest feedback.

Define eligibility, reward value, expiry, limits, and redemption clearly. Do not pay for undisclosed positive reviews or condition benefits on a favorable rating.

## Responding to negative word of mouth

- Listen without arguing reflexively.
- Verify the details and identify the underlying service failure.
- Acknowledge the customer's actual experience.
- Explain the remedy and next step.
- Correct recurring operational causes.
- Avoid exposing private customer information.
- Do not threaten, shame, or manipulate customers into removing honest criticism.

A credible response can demonstrate accountability, but it does not guarantee that the customer will change their opinion.

## Measurement

Track:
- Referral source and referred inquiries.
- Referral-to-purchase conversion.
- Referred customer quality and repeat purchase.
- Review volume, rating distribution, and review themes.
- Customer-generated content and permission status.
- Share of new customers reporting recommendations.
- Cost and contribution of referral incentives.

Separate organic recommendations from incentivized referrals. Do not count a social mention as a new customer unless the outcome is verified.

## Workflow

1. Review the customer experience and identify what is worth recommending.
2. Choose the advocacy behavior that fits the customer.
3. Remove friction from sharing or referring.
4. Explain any incentive and conditions transparently.
5. Invite feedback at an appropriate moment.
6. Track referred customers and qualitative themes.
7. Fix recurring product or service issues.
8. Improve the experience before expanding the program.

## Service recovery integration

Use [Service Recovery Marketing & Trust Building](../service-recovery-marketing-and-trust-building/SKILL.md) when a customer complaint or service failure needs an actual remedy and operational correction. A credible recovery may influence word of mouth, but compensation must never be conditional on a positive review, changed rating, or removal of criticism.
