# Video Scriptwriting & Hook Engineering

## Purpose
Turn a validated video idea into a clear, speakable script whose opening earns attention and whose body delivers the promised value. This skill owns script structure and spoken-language craft, not channel strategy or final packaging.

## Inputs
- Video objective, platform, format, target audience, and desired length.
- Core idea, evidence, examples, product facts, and intended call to action.
- Creator voice profile or representative examples.
- Any title/thumbnail promise already selected.

## Workflow
1. Confirm the viewer, problem or desire, and one outcome the video should deliver.
2. Identify the strongest truthful tension: question, misconception, contrast, consequence, demonstration, or useful promise.
3. Draft at least five meaningfully different hooks using different approaches; do not produce cosmetic rewrites.
4. Evaluate hooks for audience relevance, clarity, specificity, curiosity, credibility, and promise-delivery fit. Scores are comparative editorial judgments, not predictions of views.
5. Select the strongest two and explain the trade-off.
6. Build a spoken script: opening, context/turn, ordered beats, evidence/examples, payoff, and one primary call to action.
7. Add visual or on-screen directions only when they improve comprehension.
8. Read for natural speech, pacing, breath, jargon, and creator voice. Remove filler and unsupported claims.

## Output
- Audience and video promise.
- Five hooks with concise evaluation; top two recommended.
- Complete spoken script with section/beat labels.
- Suggested visuals where useful.
- Estimated runtime with stated speaking-rate assumption.
- Claims or facts that need verification.

## Decision rules
- The hook must be fulfilled by the video.
- Never invent statistics, quotations, case studies, or sources.
- Do not force a hook formula when the idea does not support it.
- Do not optimize only for watch time at the expense of usefulness or honesty.
- If the concept is weak or unsupported, say so and improve the premise before scripting.
