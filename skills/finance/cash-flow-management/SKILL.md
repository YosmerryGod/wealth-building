# Cash Flow Management

## Purpose

Help users plan the timing and use of money so essential obligations can be met, financial shocks can be absorbed, and business or personal goals can be funded sustainably.

## Core principles

- Track when cash is actually received and paid, not only when income is earned or expenses are incurred.
- Separate essential spending, discretionary spending, debt service, reserves, and goal-based allocations.
- Include irregular expenses by estimating their monthly equivalent.
- Match short-term obligations with sufficiently liquid funds.
- Keep business and household cash separate where practical.
- Avoid treating revenue, accounting profit, or credit availability as free cash.

## Workflow

1. Define the period and purpose: weekly household plan, monthly budget, business forecast, or project cash plan.
2. List opening cash and expected receipts by date.
3. List essential expenses, debt payments, payroll, supplier payments, taxes, and other commitments by due date.
4. Include irregular costs and a contingency allowance.
5. Calculate the projected closing balance for each period.
6. Identify the lowest projected cash point and any shortfall.
7. Compare actions: adjust timing, reduce optional costs, negotiate terms, improve collections, increase income, or delay spending.
8. Review actual cash flow against the forecast and update assumptions.

## Useful calculations

- Net cash flow = cash inflows − cash outflows.
- Closing cash = opening cash + net cash flow.
- Monthly available surplus = take-home income − essential costs − required debt payments − committed obligations.
- Cash runway = available unrestricted cash ÷ average periodic net cash burn, when burn is reasonably stable.

For businesses, separately model operating, investing, and financing cash flows where useful. Avoid double-counting transfers between accounts.

## Practical controls

- Use a calendar for bill and debt due dates.
- Maintain a realistic reserve target based on income stability, dependents, business volatility, and access to backup resources.
- Review subscriptions, fees, inventory, receivables, and payment terms.
- Monitor overdue customer invoices and supplier commitments.
- Establish a minimum cash threshold before discretionary purchases or expansion.
- Reforecast after a material change in income, costs, debt, or sales.

## Safeguards

- Do not prescribe a fixed savings percentage when the user's income does not cover essential needs.
- Do not advise skipping required payments or essential healthcare, food, housing, or safety costs to meet a savings target.
- Do not count credit limits as emergency savings.
- Do not confuse cash-flow timing problems with business profitability problems; diagnose both.
- Flag tax, insolvency, and debt distress situations for qualified local advice.
