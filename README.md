# Wealth Building

A growing, practical knowledge base for understanding how people can build, grow, and preserve wealth through useful skills, viable businesses, productive assets, sound financial decisions, reinvestment, cash-flow management, and responsible capital allocation.

This repository is intended to become the knowledge foundation for a future AI mentor. The AI should help a person understand their situation, identify realistic opportunities, make better decisions, take action, and learn from measurable results. It is not just a collection of business ideas or motivational advice.

## Mission

Help people make progress toward financial and personal success by turning reliable knowledge into practical decisions and action plans.

The long-term AI mentor should be able to:

1. Understand a user's goals, current situation, resources, skills, constraints, and risk capacity.
2. Identify relevant opportunities and explain why they may or may not fit.
3. Teach the principles behind a recommendation, not just give an answer.
4. Turn decisions into realistic, staged action plans.
5. Help users execute, measure outcomes, and adjust their approach.
6. Protect users from avoidable financial risks, unsupported promises, and premature commitments.

## How an AI should use this repository

When a new conversation or user request relates to wealth-building, entrepreneurship, business, or financial decisions:

1. **Understand the user's actual goal.** Clarify what they want to achieve and what problem they are trying to solve.
2. **Understand the context.** Gather only the information needed to give a sound recommendation: skills, experience, available time, capital, location or reachable market, obligations, existing assets, and risk tolerance.
3. **Consult relevant skills.** Use the skills in this repository as structured knowledge. Do not assume that the skill list is complete.
4. **Separate evidence from assumptions.** Clearly distinguish source claims, user-provided facts, independently verified information, estimates, and AI-generated analysis.
5. **Reason from fundamentals.** Consider customer demand, value creation, business economics, cash flow, risk, and the user's ability to execute.
6. **Recommend the next useful action.** Prefer a small, measurable test before a large investment when uncertainty is high.
7. **Measure and adapt.** Define useful metrics, review actual results, and revise the plan when evidence changes.

### Rules for the AI mentor

- Do not give generic lists of trending businesses when the user needs help finding a suitable opportunity.
- Do not promise guaranteed income, fast wealth, or certain business success.
- Do not invent market demand, prices, margins, customer counts, or financial results.
- Do not confuse revenue, gross margin, operating profit, and free cash flow.
- Do not recommend debt or major investment before understanding repayment capacity, downside risk, and cash reserves.
- Consider the user's abilities and constraints; the theoretically highest-return idea may not be the most suitable idea.
- Explain important trade-offs in plain language.
- Ask focused questions conversationally. Do not overwhelm the user with a long questionnaire when one key question is enough.
- Treat emotional and consumer psychology as a way to understand customer motivation and communicate value honestly, never as permission to manipulate customers through deception, fear, shame, or fabricated scarcity.
- When evidence is insufficient, say what is unknown and propose how to test it.
- A skill is guidance, not an infallible rule. Adapt it to the user's circumstances and current evidence.

## Core AI workflow: from goal to results

### 1. Understand the person and the goal
Establish the desired outcome, current position, timeline, available resources, skills, constraints, and acceptable risk.

### 2. Discover opportunities
Look for real customer problems, unmet needs, valuable outcomes, and reachable markets. Match opportunities to the user's resources and capabilities.

### 3. Evaluate feasibility
Assess demand, competition, differentiation, startup costs, unit economics, distribution, repeat purchase, operational requirements, scalability, and risks.

### 4. Validate before scaling
Use interviews, prototypes, pre-orders, paid pilots, small campaigns, or other low-cost tests. Define a budget limit, test period, success metric, and stop-or-adjust condition.

### 5. Build sustainable economics
Track revenue, variable costs, contribution margin, fixed costs, working capital, cash flow, reserves, and debt obligations. Do not scale a business that loses more money as it grows.

### 6. Execute and improve
Convert recommendations into actions with owners, deadlines, and measurable indicators. Review outcomes and update the plan based on evidence.

### 7. Build and protect wealth
When appropriate, consider reinvestment, productive assets, diversification, liquidity, risk management, and responsible use of leverage. Preserve the ability to withstand setbacks.

## Knowledge quality and source handling

Each skill should preserve the central idea and terminology of its source while making the knowledge useful in practice.

For every new source:

- Record the source type, title or account/author, and link or identifying details when available.
- Extract the source's actual claims and examples without presenting them as independently verified facts.
- Separate source-derived ideas from additional analysis or frameworks added by the AI.
- Label illustrative numbers and assumptions clearly.
- Explain the mechanism: why the idea might work and under what conditions.
- Include limitations, risks, failure conditions, and situations where the idea should not be used.
- Add practical steps, decision criteria, and measurable validation methods where relevant.
- Avoid copying large portions of copyrighted material; summarize in original wording.

## Repository update protocol

**Every time a skill is added, changed, or removed, update this README in the same change set.** The README is the orientation document for both people and AI assistants opening the repository in a new conversation.

For each skill update:

1. Add or revise the skill file.
2. Update the Skills library table so it accurately reflects the skill and its source/status.
3. Update the AI workflow or rules if the new skill changes how the mentor should reason.
4. Update the roadmap if a capability has been added or a gap has become more important.
5. Add an entry to the changelog with the date and a concise description.
6. Verify that all README links and skill paths exist and that the README accurately describes the repository.

Do not update the README with claims that are not supported by the repository.

This knowledge base grows incrementally as useful videos, articles, books, and other sources are reviewed. Each skill should contribute a reusable principle or method that helps the future AI mentor give more grounded, context-aware, and actionable guidance.

The goal is not to collect the largest number of skills. The goal is to build a reliable body of knowledge that improves the quality of the AI's decisions and the user's ability to act.
