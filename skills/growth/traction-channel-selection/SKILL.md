# Traction Channel Selection

## Purpose

Help an AI mentor choose customer-acquisition channels that fit a particular business instead of defaulting to familiar or fashionable platforms.

## Channel assessment

Assess each plausible channel against:

- **Audience access:** Can the target customer be reached there?
- **Intent:** Is the audience actively looking for a solution or open to discovery?
- **Offer fit:** Can the product's value be communicated in that channel?
- **Economics:** What are the likely cash and labor costs, and can the business sustain them?
- **Time to evidence:** How quickly can the business learn whether the channel works?
- **Scale:** Can the channel reach enough of the target market?
- **Repeatability:** Can results be generated consistently?
- **Measurement:** Can qualified outcomes be attributed with reasonable confidence?
- **Operational fit:** Can the business serve the demand generated?
- **Risk:** What platform dependency, policy, reputation, or compliance risks exist?

## Channel families and examples

Use the 19-channel framework from *Traction* as a discovery checklist:

- Earned attention: Public Relations, Unconventional PR, Targeting Blogs.
- Paid reach: Search Engine Marketing, Social and Display Ads, Offline Ads.
- Owned and intent-based: SEO, Content Marketing, Email Marketing.
- Product-led and network effects: Viral Marketing, Engineering as Marketing, Community Building.
- Direct and relationship-led: Sales, Business Development, Affiliate Programs.
- Ecosystem and in-person: Existing Platforms, Trade Shows, Offline Events, Speaking Engagements.

These groupings are an explanatory convenience, not a replacement for the book's individual channel distinctions.

## Decision process

1. Define the target customer and the desired conversion.
2. Map where that customer searches, learns, compares, asks for advice, and buys.
3. Identify channels that can reach the customer at the relevant stage.
4. Eliminate channels that are inaccessible, unlawful, too expensive, or operationally unsuitable.
5. Compare remaining options with explicit evidence and assumptions.
6. Select a small number for testing through the Bullseye Framework.
7. Reassess as the business gathers actual performance data.

## Business-context examples

- A local food business may test local search presence, community referrals, partnerships, signage, repeat-customer messaging, or nearby events.
- A SaaS product may test SEO, content, targeted communities, integrations, engineering-as-marketing tools, direct outreach, or referral loops.
- A creator-led product may test content, speaking, community, partnerships, and relevant existing platforms.
- A B2B service may test targeted sales, partner referrals, industry events, and specialist publications.

These are hypotheses to investigate, not universal prescriptions.

## AI decision rules

- Ask where the target customer already pays attention; do not assume the founder's preferred channel is suitable.
- Consider both cash cost and labor cost.
- Distinguish reach from qualified reach and clicks from customers.
- Check platform rules and current costs before recommending a live campaign.
- Avoid channel concentration that creates unacceptable platform dependency.
- Do not scale a channel beyond the business's ability to fulfill demand.

## Relationship to other skills

Use Bullseye Framework to brainstorm, rank, prioritize, test, and focus. Use Growth Experimentation to design tests and Growth Metrics & Optimization to assess results.

## Source limitations

The channel inventory is based on *Traction*. The channel-family groupings, assessment criteria, and business examples are additional synthesis. Channel suitability must be validated in the actual market.
