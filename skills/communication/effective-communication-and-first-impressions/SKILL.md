# Effective Communication & First Impressions

## Purpose

Help an AI mentor choose clear, respectful, context-aware communication that creates a good first impression without pretending that a technique can control another person's judgment.

## Core principles

- Communication succeeds when the intended meaning is understood, not merely when a speaker has finished talking.
- First impressions can influence later interaction, but they are revisable and should not be treated as a fixed verdict.
- Logic, relevance, credibility, and consideration for the listener strengthen a message.
- Adapt vocabulary, detail, formality, and pace to the audience and situation.
- Be concise without omitting information the listener needs to make an informed decision.

## Communication workflow

1. Identify the audience, relationship, context, and desired outcome.
2. Decide whether the goal is to inform, ask, connect, persuade, negotiate, or resolve a problem.
3. Lead with the most relevant point.
4. Support it with a clear reason, example, or evidence.
5. Use language the listener can understand.
6. Check understanding and invite questions.
7. Adjust based on the listener's response.

## First-impression checklist

- Is the opening appropriate to the relationship and setting?
- Does the speaker show respect and genuine attention?
- Are claims accurate and proportionate?
- Is the message organized and easy to follow?
- Do tone and body language fit the words?
- Is the listener given room to respond?

## Safeguards

Do not encourage fake confidence, fabricated credentials, flattery that is not genuine, or strategic concealment of relevant facts. Do not infer a person's character or intentions from one first impression.

## Relationship to other skills

Use Active Listening & Conversational Rapport for two-way interaction, Message Design & Storytelling for structuring content, Vocal & Nonverbal Delivery for delivery, and Persuasive Communication & Negotiation when the goal is to influence a decision.

### Practical communication preparation

- Clarify the purpose before choosing words: inform, ask, connect, coordinate, resolve, or negotiate.
- Adapt formality, vocabulary, detail, and channel to the relationship and setting without pretending to be someone else.
- Make the message understandable to the receiver; check what they understood rather than assuming delivery equals understanding.
- Treat communication as a social process with a sender, message, channel, receiver, context, and possible misunderstanding.
- When communication becomes tense, slow down, restate the shared issue, and clarify one point at a time.
- Practice difficult everyday conversations in low-stakes settings; review clarity and the receiver's actual response.
