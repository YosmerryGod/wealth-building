# Financial Statement Literacy

## Purpose

Teach users to read the three core financial statements and understand what they can—and cannot—reveal about a person or business.

## The three statements

### Income statement

Shows financial performance over a period:
- Revenue.
- Cost of goods sold or direct costs.
- Gross profit.
- Operating expenses.
- Operating profit.
- Interest, tax, and net profit.

### Balance sheet

Shows financial position at a point in time:
- Assets.
- Liabilities.
- Equity.

The basic accounting equation is: Assets = Liabilities + Equity.

### Cash flow statement

Explains changes in cash through:
- Operating activities.
- Investing activities.
- Financing activities.

Profit and cash movement can differ because of receivables, inventory, payables, depreciation, capital expenditure, debt principal, and timing.

## Reading workflow

1. Confirm entity, reporting period, currency, accounting basis, and whether figures are audited.
2. Check completeness and consistency of statements.
3. Review revenue and gross-margin trends.
4. Examine operating expenses and operating profit.
5. Review assets, liabilities, working capital, and debt maturity.
6. Compare net profit with operating cash flow.
7. Identify unusual items, concentration, seasonality, and changes in accounting policy.
8. State what additional notes or records are needed before drawing conclusions.

## Useful ratios

Use ratios only when the underlying definitions and data are comparable:
- Gross margin = (revenue − cost of goods sold) ÷ revenue.
- Operating margin = operating profit ÷ revenue.
- Current ratio = current assets ÷ current liabilities.
- Debt-to-equity = total debt or liabilities ÷ equity, with the chosen definition stated.
- Receivables days and inventory days, where data supports them.

Explain industry context and limitations. A ratio alone does not establish financial health.

## Common mistakes

- Calling revenue "profit".
- Treating depreciation as a cash payment in the period.
- Ignoring capital expenditure and working capital.
- Comparing companies with different accounting policies without adjustment.
- Assuming positive net income means sufficient cash to pay bills.
- Treating an incomplete or user-entered report as audited.

## Safeguards

Do not fabricate missing figures or present educational analysis as an audit. Protect confidential financial information. Refer tax, statutory reporting, insolvency, and complex accounting judgments to qualified professionals.

## Relationship to other skills

Use Cash Flow Management for forward-looking liquidity, Asset & Liability Analysis for asset classification, and Financial Intelligence to connect statements to decisions.
