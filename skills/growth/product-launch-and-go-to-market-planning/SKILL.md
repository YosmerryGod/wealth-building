# Product Launch & Go-to-Market Planning

## Purpose
Translate a validated product and differentiated positioning into a credible launch plan, customer-facing assets, initial distribution, and measurable learning. A launch is a staged market test, not proof of product-market fit.

## Inputs
Use real product capabilities, target customer, validated problem, differentiation, brand system, readiness, pricing constraints, available proof, channel access, budget, and support capacity. Separate verified evidence from hypotheses.

## Method
1. **Readiness/positioning:** state who the product serves, the problem, and the concrete outcome. Lead with customer benefit, not copied feature lists. Verify advertised capabilities; identify objections and available proof. Never invent users, testimonials, ratings, revenue, or press.
2. **Landing page:** outline hero/value proposition, problem, how it works, capabilities tied to outcomes, credible proof, offer/pricing, FAQs, and clear CTA. Use the product's own voice and assets.
3. **Pricing inputs:** research dated public competitor prices only when requested and available; record URL, currency, billing period, plan limits, and date. Delegate calculations to Finance. Marketplace calculations must preserve the AYDO source formula.
4. **Offer/proof:** define beta, waitlist, trial, free tier, or paid launch only when economics and support capacity fit. State eligibility, duration, limits, renewal, cancellation, and data handling. Obtain permission for customer proof.
5. **Distribution:** choose a small number of channels based on audience access, cost, feedback speed, and support capacity. Coordinate with Traction Strategy, Channel Selection, Bullseye, and Growth Experimentation when needed.
6. **Store claims:** verify current platform rules at submission time. Keep names, descriptions, screenshots, and claims accurate; do not imply affiliation with a reference product.
7. **Metrics:** choose a compact scorecard tied to the launch goal (qualified visits, activation, core-task completion, conversion, retention, support burden, refund/cancellation signals). Define event, denominator, time window, and owner.

## Output
Launch brief with positioning, audience, readiness, landing-page outline, offer/pricing inputs, channel sequence, store checklist, evidence/claims audit, metrics, risks, and next learning milestone.

## Relationships
Business Opportunity Discovery owns customer/demand evidence. Professional Logo & Brand Identity owns brand identity. Finance owns pricing calculations. Traction/Growth own acquisition strategy and tests. Deployment & Release Engineering owns technical release.
