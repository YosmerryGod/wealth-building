# Envelope Budgeting System

## Purpose

Help a person or household assign available money to clear spending purposes, control category-level spending, and reduce the risk of using money reserved for bills, savings, or future obligations.

## Core concept

An envelope is a named allocation of money for a specific purpose and period. It may be a physical cash envelope, a bank sub-account, a digital wallet balance, or a tracked budget category. The method is about separating purposes, not requiring cash.

Common envelopes include:
- Housing and utilities.
- Food and household essentials.
- Transport.
- Health and dependents.
- Debt payments.
- Irregular expenses and sinking funds.
- Emergency reserves.
- Personal and discretionary spending.
- Long-term goals.

## Budgeting methods and variations

### Category/envelope budgeting
Assign a spending limit to each category before spending. Track actual spending against each envelope.

### Zero-based budgeting
Assign every unit of expected income a job: spending, obligations, savings, debt reduction, or reserves. A zero remainder means all income has been allocated, not that the bank balance must be zero.

### Digital and cash-envelope systems
Use separate accounts, wallet pockets, spreadsheet categories, or an app. Cash may help some users see limits clearly, while digital methods may improve safety, traceability, and bill payment convenience.

### Flexible rollover
Allow unused money to roll into the next period when the category represents a continuing goal or irregular cost. Alternatively, sweep genuine surplus toward a priority after checking upcoming obligations.

## Operating workflow

1. Establish the budget period and reliable take-home income.
2. List essential bills, required debt payments, and contractual obligations with due dates.
3. Identify variable essentials and set realistic category limits from actual history.
4. Create sinking funds for predictable non-monthly costs such as school fees, annual renewals, repairs, or holidays.
5. Set a feasible emergency-reserve contribution and other goal allocations.
6. Allocate the remaining amount to discretionary categories.
7. Check that total allocations do not exceed available income and opening cash.
8. Track transactions against the correct envelope.
9. Review category balances during the period, not only at month-end.
10. Reallocate only after checking the consequences for other envelopes and future obligations.

## Decision rules

- Fund essentials and required obligations before optional categories.
- Treat irregular but predictable expenses as planned costs, not emergencies.
- Do not use the emergency reserve for routine overspending.
- If an envelope is exhausted, pause that category, reduce another genuinely flexible category, or revise the plan; do not silently borrow from rent, food, debt payments, or other protected needs.
- Permit reasonable transfers between categories when circumstances change. The system should support decisions, not become a rigid punishment.
- For irregular income, budget from a conservative baseline and allocate extra receipts only after current obligations and buffers are covered.
- Keep business and household envelopes separate where practical.

## Calculations

**Available to allocate** = opening available cash + reliable income expected during the period − required obligations due during the period.

**Envelope remaining** = allocated amount + permitted rollover − actual spending.

**Sinking-fund contribution per period** = (target cost − current amount saved) ÷ number of periods remaining.

**Budget variance** = actual spending − planned spending.

Explain whether a positive variance is overspending or underspending according to the metric used.

## Review metrics

- Share of categories within budget.
- Essential-bill payment reliability.
- Frequency and size of transfers between envelopes.
- Unplanned borrowing and overdraft events.
- Sinking-fund coverage for upcoming known costs.
- Emergency-fund withdrawals and reasons.
- Difference between planned and actual spending.

## Common failure modes

- Unrealistic category limits based on aspiration rather than actual spending.
- Forgetting annual, seasonal, or irregular costs.
- Too many categories, creating maintenance fatigue.
- Treating transfers between accounts as new income or spending.
- Using credit-card limits as available budget.
- Making the system so strict that one unexpected expense causes abandonment.
- Allocating income that has not actually arrived or is uncertain.

## AI workflow

When helping a user build an envelope budget:
1. Determine whether income is stable, variable, seasonal, or irregular.
2. Establish the budget period, household size, essential needs, due dates, debt commitments, and current cash.
3. Use transaction history where available; label estimates when history is missing.
4. Build a small set of meaningful envelopes first.
5. Include sinking funds and reserve categories separately.
6. Check arithmetic and cash timing before recommending transfers.
7. Explain what to do when a category is exhausted.
8. Review actual results and adjust limits without shaming the user.
