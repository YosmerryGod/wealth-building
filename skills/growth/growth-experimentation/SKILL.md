# Growth Experimentation

## Purpose

Help an AI mentor test growth hypotheses cheaply and responsibly before recommending larger commitments of time, money, or people.

## Experiment design

Every experiment should specify:

1. **Business objective:** The business result the experiment is intended to improve.
2. **Hypothesis:** A falsifiable statement about audience, channel, offer, and expected behavior.
3. **Audience:** Who is included and who is excluded.
4. **Intervention:** The exact message, offer, product change, campaign, or sales activity.
5. **Baseline:** Current performance or the best available reference point.
6. **Metric:** The primary outcome and relevant guardrail metrics.
7. **Budget and effort:** Maximum cash spend and staff time.
8. **Duration and sample:** A reasonable observation window and sample requirement.
9. **Decision threshold:** What result would justify stopping, changing, repeating, or expanding.
10. **Owner and next action:** Who will execute and when the result will be reviewed.

## Experiment cycle

- Observe a growth bottleneck.
- Form one clear hypothesis.
- Choose the smallest test that can produce useful evidence.
- Run the test without changing multiple major variables unnecessarily.
- Record outcomes and unusual conditions.
- Compare results with the baseline and threshold.
- Decide: stop, iterate, repeat, or scale.
- Document the learning for future experiments.

## Choosing metrics

Match the metric to the stage:
- Awareness: qualified reach, relevant visits, branded search, or target-account engagement.
- Acquisition: qualified leads, sign-ups, or first-time buyers.
- Activation: completion of a meaningful first-use action.
- Revenue: paid conversion, order value, and contribution margin.
- Retention: repeat purchase, cohort retention, or continued meaningful use.
- Referral: referred qualified users and their conversion.

Use guardrails such as refund rate, complaint rate, churn, fulfillment quality, and cash consumption. Do not optimize a metric in a way that damages the customer experience or long-term value.

## Sample-size and uncertainty rules

- Do not claim statistical certainty from a tiny sample.
- For low-volume businesses, combine quantitative results with customer interviews and observed behavior.
- Consider sales-cycle length; a short test may miss delayed conversions.
- Separate correlation from causation.
- Record external factors such as promotions, seasonality, outages, and competitor activity.
- If the result is inconclusive, say so and propose the next smallest useful test.

## AI decision rules

- Never invent test results or imply an experiment was run when it was only planned.
- Never spend, publish, message customers, or change live systems without the user's explicit authorization.
- Set a loss limit before paid tests.
- Prefer reversible tests before irreversible commitments.
- Protect privacy and comply with applicable platform rules and laws.
- Avoid deceptive creative, fake testimonials, spam, and manipulative urgency.
