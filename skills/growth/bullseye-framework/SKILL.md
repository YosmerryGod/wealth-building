# Bullseye Framework

## Purpose

Help an AI mentor systematically discover which customer-acquisition channel is most likely to create meaningful traction for a specific business.

## The five stages

### 1. Brainstorm

Consider the full set of 19 traction channels. For each channel, ask how it could reach the target customer, what campaign or mechanism could be used, what resources it would require, and what evidence would make it promising.

The 19 channels are:

1. Viral Marketing
2. Public Relations
3. Unconventional PR
4. Search Engine Marketing
5. Social and Display Ads
6. Offline Ads
7. Search Engine Optimization
8. Content Marketing
9. Email Marketing
10. Engineering as Marketing
11. Targeting Blogs
12. Business Development
13. Sales
14. Affiliate Programs
15. Existing Platforms
16. Trade Shows
17. Offline Events
18. Speaking Engagements
19. Community Building

Not every channel is relevant to every business. Do not force a channel simply to complete the list.

### 2. Rank

Rank channels using explicit criteria:
- Audience fit and reachability.
- Likely customer intent and quality.
- Cost, time, and operational effort.
- Speed to useful evidence.
- Potential scale and repeatability.
- Fit with the offer and customer journey.
- Measurement feasibility and downside risk.

Explain the reasons for the ranking. Avoid false precision when evidence is weak.

### 3. Prioritize

Select a small number of channels—commonly three—for deeper investigation or initial tests. The selection should balance promising reach, plausible economics, and the ability to run a meaningful experiment.

### 4. Test

Design a bounded experiment for each selected channel. Define the hypothesis, audience, offer, creative or message, budget, duration, success metric, minimum evidence threshold, and stop condition.

### 5. Focus

Compare actual results and concentrate effort on the channel or combination of channels that creates qualified customers at sustainable economics. Continue learning; do not treat one short test as conclusive when the sample is too small or the sales cycle is long.

## Experiment comparison

Use a consistent record:

| Field | What to record |
|---|---|
| Channel | The channel being tested |
| Hypothesis | Why it may reach and convert the audience |
| Audience | Intended customer segment |
| Test | Exact campaign or activity |
| Cost | Cash cost and meaningful labor/time cost |
| Reach | Relevant people reached, not just raw impressions |
| Outcome | Leads, activated users, paid customers, or other goal |
| Economics | Acquisition cost and contribution where measurable |
| Learning | What the test supports or disproves |
| Decision | Stop, modify, repeat, or scale |

## AI decision rules

- Brainstorm broadly, but do not test all channels at once.
- Rank based on the user's actual customer, offer, resources, and market—not generic popularity.
- Do not call a channel a winner based only on impressions, likes, or cheap clicks.
- Account for attribution uncertainty, delayed conversions, seasonality, and small samples.
- Preserve test records so future decisions use accumulated evidence.
- If all tests fail, revisit the offer, audience, positioning, and customer problem before merely increasing spend.
