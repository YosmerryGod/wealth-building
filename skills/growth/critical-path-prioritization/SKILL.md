# Critical Path & Growth Prioritization

## Purpose

Help an AI mentor identify the current constraint that most limits customer acquisition or business growth, then prioritize the next action that can remove or validate that constraint.

## Core principle

Growth work is not a list of equally important tasks. A business may have many possible improvements, but the next priority should address the constraint that currently prevents the desired outcome.

Examples:
- If customers do not understand the offer, more traffic may not help.
- If visitors arrive but do not activate, improve onboarding or product value before buying more traffic.
- If customers buy but do not return, investigate product quality, fit, and experience before aggressively acquiring more customers.
- If demand exceeds capacity, improve fulfillment before increasing promotion.

## Diagnostic sequence

1. Define the outcome and the current baseline.
2. Map the customer journey from discovery to repeat purchase or referral.
3. Locate the largest evidenced drop-off or operational bottleneck.
4. Identify the likely root cause and what evidence is missing.
5. Choose one action or experiment that addresses the bottleneck.
6. Estimate impact, effort, cost, risk, and time to evidence.
7. Set an owner, deadline, and measurable completion condition.
8. Review the result and identify the next constraint.

## Prioritization criteria

Consider:
- Expected impact on the business objective.
- Confidence in the diagnosis.
- Effort and cash cost.
- Time to useful evidence.
- Reversibility and downside.
- Dependencies and fulfillment capacity.

A simple qualitative ranking (high, medium, low) is often more honest than a precise score when data is limited. If a numerical score is used, show the criteria and assumptions.

## AI decision rules

- Do not mistake activity for progress.
- Do not recommend adding traffic when the offer, activation, retention, or fulfillment is the actual constraint.
- Do not treat a speculative bottleneck as a proven fact.
- Ask for the smallest missing piece of evidence that could change the priority.
- Keep a short list of next actions; avoid overwhelming the user with an unranked backlog.
- Revisit priorities when new evidence changes the constraint.
