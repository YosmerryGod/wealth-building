# Traction Strategy

## Purpose

Help an AI mentor design a repeatable, evidence-led customer acquisition strategy. The skill translates the central idea of *Traction* into an operating process: a business needs a dependable way to reach customers, and the best channel must be discovered through structured testing rather than assumption.

## Core principle

A product can be useful and still fail to grow if its intended customers do not discover it. Treat distribution and customer acquisition as core business activities, not tasks postponed until after product development.

Traction is not the same as vanity attention. Views, impressions, followers, downloads, and press mentions are useful only when connected to the intended outcome, such as qualified leads, activated users, paid customers, repeat purchases, or sustainable revenue.

## Operating process

1. **Define the growth outcome.** Clarify the target customer, desired action, baseline, target, time horizon, and constraints.
2. **Understand the customer journey.** Identify how customers discover, evaluate, buy, activate, use, and recommend the offer.
3. **Map possible channels.** Consider all relevant traction channels rather than defaulting to the founder's favorite platform.
4. **Generate channel hypotheses.** State which audience can be reached, why the channel may work, what offer or message will be tested, and what evidence would support the hypothesis.
5. **Prioritize and test.** Use the Bullseye Framework and Growth Experimentation skills to select a small number of promising tests.
6. **Measure business outcomes.** Track qualified acquisition, activation, conversion, retention, contribution margin, and acquisition cost where data permits.
7. **Concentrate resources.** Invest more in channels that show repeatable, economically sound results. Keep testing when the market or channel changes.
8. **Review and adapt.** Reassess the strategy when customer behavior, competition, platform rules, product positioning, or unit economics change.

## Strategic questions

- Who is the exact customer and where can they realistically be reached?
- What problem or desired outcome makes the offer worth considering?
- What alternatives does the customer use now?
- Which channels provide access to this audience?
- What proof would show that a channel creates qualified customers rather than empty attention?
- Can the business fulfill additional demand without damaging quality or cash flow?
- Is the acquisition cost sustainable relative to contribution margin and customer value?

## AI decision rules

- Do not assume that social media is the best channel for every business.
- Do not recommend scaling spend before the offer, conversion path, and fulfillment capacity are credible.
- Distinguish awareness, lead generation, activation, purchase, retention, and referral.
- Use current research when channel costs, platform rules, audience behavior, or competitor activity may have changed.
- Label assumptions and estimates. Never invent conversion rates, customer counts, or acquisition costs.
- Prefer a small test with a clear stop condition when uncertainty is high.
- Protect customer trust: do not recommend fake reviews, fabricated popularity, deceptive scarcity, spam, or misleading claims.
