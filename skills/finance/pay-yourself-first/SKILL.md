# Pay Yourself First

## Purpose

Help users create a repeatable way to allocate part of available income toward savings and long-term goals before discretionary spending, without neglecting essential needs or contractual obligations.

## Principle

"Pay yourself first" is a behavioral budgeting principle: decide in advance how much of genuinely available income should go toward reserves or goals, then separate it so it is not casually consumed.

It is not an instruction to ignore rent, food, healthcare, taxes, payroll, or required debt payments. The allocation must fit the user's real cash-flow position.

## Implementation

1. Calculate reliable take-home income and its timing.
2. Identify essential living or operating costs and required obligations.
3. Account for irregular expenses and near-term cash needs.
4. Identify high-interest debt and urgent arrears that may deserve priority.
5. Set a sustainable reserve or goal contribution.
6. Automate a transfer only when timing and balances make it safe.
7. Keep emergency reserves separate from investment capital.
8. Review the amount when income, expenses, dependents, or goals change.

## Allocation order to consider

The order is contextual, but often includes:
- Essential needs and safety.
- Required legal, tax, and contractual obligations.
- Minimum debt payments and urgent arrears.
- A starter emergency reserve.
- High-cost debt reduction where appropriate.
- Additional emergency savings and sinking funds.
- Long-term savings or diversified investments consistent with the user's horizon and risk capacity.

This is a decision sequence, not a universal ranking. Compare the guaranteed cost of debt with uncertain investment returns and preserve enough liquidity to avoid repeated borrowing.

## Measurement

Track:
- Amount and percentage allocated.
- Consistency across pay periods.
- Emergency reserve coverage.
- High-interest debt balance.
- Progress toward named goals.
- Whether the plan causes missed bills or new borrowing.

## Safeguards

- Never shame a user who cannot save.
- Do not set arbitrary percentages without understanding income and obligations.
- Do not invest money needed for near-term essentials or emergency liquidity.
- Do not recommend ignoring high-interest debt or required payments.
- Do not present savings as sufficient on its own; income growth, cost structure, risk, and access to opportunity also matter.

## Relationship to other skills

Use Cash Flow Management to determine affordability, Financial Intelligence to compare priorities, and Income-to-Asset Wealth Building to plan how accumulated capital may be used productively.
