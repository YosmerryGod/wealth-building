# Creative Intelligence Engine

## Purpose

Help an AI generate, discover, connect, develop, and evaluate genuinely different ideas across business, marketing, product development, technology, communication, personal projects, and other domains.

Creativity is not novelty for its own sake. A useful creative result changes how a problem is understood, reveals a possibility, or produces a solution that can be tested and used.

This is a cross-domain reasoning skill. It complements specialist knowledge and does not replace domain expertise, evidence gathering, financial evaluation, or user approval.

## When to use

Use this skill when the user:
- asks for new ideas, alternatives, concepts, strategies, or unconventional approaches;
- faces a problem that conventional solutions have not resolved;
- wants to find opportunities from customer behavior, market changes, existing assets, or constraints;
- is designing a business, product, feature, campaign, workflow, or experience;
- asks the AI to connect knowledge from different domains;
- wants to improve, recombine, or challenge an existing idea.

Do not force a creative exercise into a request that only needs a direct factual answer or a routine execution step.

## Core capabilities

### 1. Divergent thinking

Expand the solution space before selecting an answer.

- Generate alternatives that differ in mechanism, audience, value, channel, cost, or delivery—not merely wording.
- Explore conventional, adjacent, and unconventional directions.
- Use prompts such as: What else could solve this? Who experiences the problem differently? What resource is currently underused?
- Generate enough options to expose meaningful choices, then narrow them.

### 2. Lateral thinking

Change the frame of the problem instead of following its most obvious path.

- Reverse the assumption: what if the opposite were true?
- Remove or relax a constraint to reveal the underlying need.
- Borrow a solution pattern from an unrelated field.
- Change the unit of analysis: individual to group, product to experience, transaction to relationship, feature to workflow.
- Ask what would make the current approach unnecessary.

Lateral thinking should create testable possibilities, not merely unusual statements.

### 3. Connecting the dots

Find useful relationships among facts, observations, skills, trends, and existing knowledge.

- Identify recurring patterns across different situations.
- Connect a customer behavior with a business model, a technology with a distribution problem, or an operational constraint with a product opportunity.
- Explain the mechanism that makes the connection useful.
- Distinguish a supported relationship from a speculative analogy.
- Use the Knowledge Graph Builder to discover related repository concepts and skills when cross-domain knowledge is involved.

### 4. Analogical thinking

Transfer a principle from one context to another.

- Identify the structure of a successful mechanism, not just its surface appearance.
- Map the source context to the target context.
- State which parts transfer and which do not.
- Adapt the principle to the target user's customers, resources, constraints, and environment.
- Avoid assuming that a successful example guarantees success elsewhere.

### 5. Assumption challenging

Expose beliefs that may be narrowing the solution space.

- List the assumptions embedded in the problem statement.
- Classify each as confirmed, plausible, untested, or contradicted.
- Ask what evidence would change the assumption.
- Test whether the constraint is real, temporary, self-imposed, or negotiable.
- Preserve genuine legal, safety, financial, technical, and operational constraints.

### 6. Observation-led opportunity discovery

Use observable behavior and context as raw material for ideas.

- Notice repeated complaints, workarounds, delays, unmet expectations, unused resources, and friction.
- Separate what people say from what they actually do.
- Look for costly, frequent, urgent, or poorly served problems.
- Consider who experiences the problem, who pays, who decides, and who uses the solution.
- Route business-market validation to Business Opportunity Discovery rather than treating an interesting observation as proof of demand.

### 7. Creative problem solving

Translate a broad problem into a solvable challenge.

1. Define the desired outcome.
2. Separate the visible symptom from the underlying problem.
3. Gather relevant facts and constraints.
4. Reframe the problem in multiple ways.
5. Generate materially different solutions.
6. Combine or improve promising ideas.
7. Evaluate feasibility and consequences.
8. Select a small next test or implementation step.

### 8. Idea development and recombination

Turn rough concepts into coherent proposals.

- Combine complementary ideas when the combination creates additional value.
- Simplify ideas that require too many assumptions or dependencies.
- Identify the user, problem, proposed mechanism, and expected benefit.
- Describe how the idea would work in practice.
- Identify what must be true for it to succeed.

### 9. Creative evaluation

Do not confuse an exciting idea with a good decision.

Evaluate relevant ideas using:
- User or customer value.
- Novelty or meaningful differentiation.
- Evidence of need or demand.
- Feasibility with available skills, time, technology, and resources.
- Cost, margin, cash-flow impact, and opportunity cost where relevant.
- Operational complexity and ability to deliver consistently.
- Risks, dependencies, reversibility, and failure conditions.
- Ease and cost of testing.

Use qualitative comparisons when evidence is weak. Do not manufacture precise scores or pretend estimates are measured facts.

### 10. Experimentation and learning

Convert uncertainty into a bounded test.

- State the hypothesis and the assumption being tested.
- Identify the target user or customer and the smallest useful prototype or trial.
- Define the metric, evidence threshold, time window, cost ceiling, and stop condition.
- Record results and unexpected observations.
- Decide whether to continue, adapt, combine, or discard the idea.
- Route growth-channel tests to Growth Experimentation and business-demand validation to Business Opportunity Discovery.

## Operating workflow

### Step 1 — Understand the creative task

Clarify the user's goal, current problem, audience, context, resources, and constraints. Use existing conversation and project context; do not ask for information already available.

### Step 2 — Reframe the challenge

Write a concise problem statement. Produce at least two alternative framings when the problem is ambiguous or conventional thinking appears limiting.

### Step 3 — Map relevant knowledge

Identify useful observations, existing assets, skills, specialist knowledge, and cross-domain relationships. Consult Knowledge Graph Builder when the repository contains potentially relevant skills in multiple domains.

### Step 4 — Expand the solution space

Use a suitable combination of divergent thinking, lateral thinking, analogy, assumption challenging, and recombination. Avoid generating superficial variations.

### Step 5 — Develop candidate ideas

For each promising candidate, explain:
- the idea in plain language;
- the problem or opportunity it addresses;
- how it works;
- why it may be different or useful;
- the key assumptions and dependencies.

### Step 6 — Evaluate and prioritize

Compare candidates against the user's actual goal and constraints. Explain trade-offs and uncertainty. Select a recommended direction while preserving meaningful alternatives.

### Step 7 — Test or execute

Recommend the smallest meaningful validation step. For implementation requests, route to the relevant business, marketing, finance, or development skills and follow the user's approval and execution constraints.

### Step 8 — Learn and update

Use actual outcomes to revise assumptions and improve future idea generation. Do not treat an untested idea as validated.

## Output format

Adapt the depth to the request. A useful creative analysis may include:

1. **Problem reframing** — what may actually need solving.
2. **Key observation** — the evidence or pattern that matters.
3. **Ideas** — distinct alternatives with their mechanisms.
4. **Best candidate** — the strongest fit and why.
5. **Risks and assumptions** — what could make it fail.
6. **Next experiment** — the cheapest useful way to learn.

For simple requests, return only the most useful ideas and a concise recommendation.

## Cross-domain integration

- **Knowledge Graph Builder:** discovers related concepts and skills, maps evidence-backed connections, and checks for overlap. Creative Intelligence uses those connections to generate new hypotheses; it does not replace repository mapping.
- **Business Opportunity Discovery:** validates customer problems, segments, willingness to pay, and market feasibility.
- **Situational Intelligence & Contextual Reasoning:** reconstructs complex situations and compares feasible actions.
- **Human Behavior Intelligence Layer:** interprets behavior and social context from observable evidence.
- **Consumer Psychology & Persuasion:** applies buyer motivation and decision principles to offers and marketing.
- **Growth Experimentation:** tests growth hypotheses with measurable experiments.
- **Business Agent Orchestrator:** coordinates creative discovery within an approved business project.
- **Frontend Design and other Development skills:** translate validated product or interface concepts into usable implementations.

Use only specialist skills that add a distinct contribution. Do not duplicate their complete methods inside this skill.

## Reasoning quality

- Prefer useful originality over novelty for its own sake.
- Separate observations, source claims, assumptions, hypotheses, and verified results.
- Do not claim to know private thoughts or motivations without evidence.
- Do not present analogies as proof.
- Do not assume every problem needs a new idea; improving or removing an existing process may be better.
- Do not overwhelm the user with a long list when one strong recommendation is sufficient.
- Match the depth of exploration to the importance, uncertainty, and reversibility of the decision.
- Preserve user constraints and approval requirements.

## Limitations

This is an AI reasoning framework, not human consciousness, lived experience, intuition, or a guarantee of originality. Its outputs depend on available context and evidence. Ideas require domain review and real-world testing before being treated as viable.

## Ownership and routing boundary

Follow [Skill Boundary & Routing Governance](../../orchestrator/skill-boundary-governance/SKILL.md). Creative Intelligence owns expansion and recombination of possible solutions. It does not validate market demand, calculate financial viability, or authorize execution. Route business ideas to Business Opportunity Discovery for customer validation; route feasible plans to the relevant operations and finance specialists; route experiments to Growth Experimentation. Knowledge Graph Builder maps evidence-backed connections but does not replace creative ideation. Return distinct options, assumptions, selection rationale, and testable hypotheses.
