# Title & Thumbnail Packaging Optimization

## Purpose
Design the YouTube click package as one coordinated promise: title and thumbnail should complement rather than repeat each other. Own packaging quality, not scriptwriting or analytics attribution.

## Inputs
Video premise, audience, finished or planned content, primary viewer benefit, evidence, channel context, and format/device considerations.

## Workflow
1. State the actual value and strongest proof in the video.
2. Generate 8–10 distinct title directions: specific outcome, question, contrast, mistake, demonstration, or timely relevance where truthful.
3. Draft a thumbnail concept for each finalist. The image should add a visual question or proof, not restate the title.
4. Check clarity, specificity, relevance, credibility, truncation risk, mobile readability, and consistency with the actual video.
5. Shortlist three packages and explain why each may appeal to a different viewer motivation.
6. Recommend one package and a test hypothesis. Do not claim a guaranteed CTR improvement.

## Evaluation rubric
- Promise is clear and accurate.
- Viewer can understand the topic quickly.
- Title and thumbnail contribute different information.
- Visual hierarchy survives small-screen display.
- No fabricated urgency, result, authority, or before/after.
- Packaging attracts the intended audience, not merely broad curiosity.

## Output
A table of title, thumbnail visual/text direction, combined promise, strengths, risks, and recommended test. Include the chosen package and what the video must deliver.

## Decision rules
- Avoid title-thumbnail duplication.
- Use minimal thumbnail text; no fixed character or word limit is universal.
- Never use misleading imagery or claims.
- Treat CTR in context with impressions, audience source, and retention.
- If the content does not support the promise, revise the promise or the content.
