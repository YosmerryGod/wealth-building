# Emergency Fund Strategy

## Purpose

Help users build, hold, use, and replenish liquid reserves for genuine financial shocks without confusing emergency savings with routine spending, predictable expenses, or investment capital.

## What an emergency fund is

An emergency fund is accessible money reserved for unexpected, necessary, and time-sensitive costs or income disruption. It provides time and choice when normal cash flow is interrupted.

Examples can include:
- Sudden loss or interruption of income.
- Urgent medical or essential household costs.
- Necessary vehicle or equipment repair that protects income or safety.
- Essential home repairs after an unexpected event.
- Urgent business continuity costs, when held separately from household reserves.

Predictable annual bills, planned purchases, holidays, routine maintenance, and known seasonal expenses are generally better handled through sinking funds or ordinary budget categories.

## Reserve sizing

Do not apply a universal target without context. Estimate a reserve range using:
- Essential monthly expenses.
- Income stability and number of income sources.
- Dependents and household responsibilities.
- Health, housing, transport, and business exposure.
- Insurance coverage, deductibles, and exclusions.
- Availability and reliability of other support.
- Time likely needed to replace income.
- Liquidity and access to funds.

A practical starting calculation is:

**Monthly essential burn** = essential housing + food + utilities + transport + healthcare + dependents + minimum required debt payments + other unavoidable costs.

**Reserve coverage in months** = liquid emergency reserve ÷ monthly essential burn.

Use scenario ranges rather than claiming one number is right for everyone. A stable dual-income household may have a different need from a single-income household, a freelancer, or a seasonal business owner.

## Building the reserve

1. Confirm that essential bills and minimum required debt payments are current.
2. Identify a starter buffer that can handle a modest common shock.
3. Set a contribution that fits genuine available surplus.
4. Keep the reserve separate from daily spending and investment accounts.
5. Prefer reliable access, capital preservation, and low volatility over return maximization.
6. Increase the target when income becomes less stable, obligations grow, or risk exposure changes.
7. Review the reserve after major life, employment, debt, insurance, or business changes.

Automated transfers can help when cash flow is reliable, but they should not trigger overdrafts or missed essentials.

## Where to hold it

Evaluate the account or instrument by:
- Safety of principal.
- Access speed and withdrawal conditions.
- Deposit protection and institution risk where relevant.
- Fees, limits, and operational reliability.
- Currency and inflation exposure.
- Whether the user can access it during the type of disruption being planned for.

Do not treat volatile investments, locked products, credit limits, or speculative assets as equivalent to cash reserves. The emergency fund's primary job is resilience, not maximum yield.

## Withdrawal policy

Before using the fund, ask:
1. Is the expense unexpected or materially uncertain?
2. Is it necessary or important to health, safety, housing, or income continuity?
3. Is it urgent enough that delaying would materially worsen the outcome?
4. Is there a more appropriate sinking fund, insurance claim, warranty, or ordinary budget category?
5. What amount is actually required?

A genuine emergency can justify using the reserve. Avoid shaming the user for making a necessary withdrawal.

## Replenishment

After a withdrawal:
- Recalculate remaining reserve coverage.
- Restore the buffer through a feasible schedule.
- Temporarily redirect optional savings or discretionary surplus if appropriate.
- Revisit spending, income, insurance, and exposure if repeated emergencies occur.
- Do not create new high-cost debt merely to rebuild a reserve at an unrealistic speed.

## Special cases

### Irregular income
Build reserves during stronger earning periods and budget from a conservative baseline. Consider separate tax, operating, and personal reserves where relevant.

### Self-employed people and business owners
Separate household emergency savings from business working capital, tax reserves, inventory funding, and equipment-repair reserves. These funds serve different purposes.

### High-interest debt
Compare the cost of debt with the value of liquidity. A starter reserve may prevent new borrowing, while aggressive repayment may be appropriate after a basic buffer exists. The right sequence depends on rates, arrears, minimum payments, and risk.

## AI workflow

1. Calculate essential monthly burn from confirmed or clearly estimated figures.
2. Identify existing liquid reserves and restrictions on access.
3. Map income volatility, dependents, debt, insurance, and major exposures.
4. Propose a starter target and a longer-term target range with explicit assumptions.
5. Select a safe and accessible holding arrangement based on the user's jurisdiction and available products.
6. Define qualifying withdrawals and a replenishment plan.
7. Review coverage after material changes.

## Metrics

- Reserve balance.
- Months of essential-expense coverage.
- Time required to access funds.
- Contributions and withdrawals by reason.
- Replenishment progress.
- Frequency of emergency borrowing.

## Limitations

This skill provides planning education, not a guarantee against financial shocks. Deposit protection, tax treatment, insurance, and suitable cash products vary by jurisdiction and must be verified locally.
